# Refinement quality rubric

Run this check before delivery. Do not expose scores unless the user asks.

## 1. Fidelity

- Does the output preserve the source's purpose and position?
- Are facts, numbers, names, dates, conditions, and qualifiers intact?
- Are citations, links, quotations, and attribution preserved?
- Are additions clearly identified as inference, recommendation, or placeholder?

Fail if the rewrite sounds better but changes what the source commits to or claims.

## 2. Completeness

- Are essential constraints, dependencies, exceptions, risks, and requested actions present?
- Did compression remove information that could change a decision?
- Are disagreements or contradictions still visible?

Fail if a reader could act incorrectly because important context disappeared.

## 3. Logic

- Is the thesis or requested outcome clear?
- Does each conclusion follow from stated evidence or assumptions?
- Are transitions and levels of abstraction coherent?
- Are alternatives and tradeoffs represented fairly when relevant?

Fail if the text is fluent but the reasoning still jumps or circles.

## 4. Structure

- Does the order match the reader's likely questions?
- Is the conclusion, request, or next step easy to locate?
- Are headings and lists doing real navigational work?
- Is supporting detail placed after the main point?

Fail if the reader must reconstruct the hierarchy.

## 5. Clarity and economy

- Are sentences precise and appropriately short?
- Are vague pronouns, empty intensifiers, clichés, and duplicated points removed?
- Is terminology consistent?
- Is jargon either necessary or explained?

Fail if the text is shorter but denser, or smoother but less specific.

## 6. Audience and tone

- Does the output assume the right level of knowledge?
- Does tone fit the relationship, channel, and stakes?
- Does it preserve the author's useful voice?
- Is uncertainty expressed at the correct strength?

Fail if the text becomes generic, patronizing, overconfident, or inappropriately formal.

## 7. Usability

- Can the reader understand what matters within the expected reading time?
- Are decisions, actions, owners, deadlines, and open questions explicit when sourced?
- Is the requested format satisfied?
- Can the result be used without another editing pass?

Fail if the output is mainly commentary about how the source could be improved.

## Final adversarial check

Ask:

1. What important meaning could the author say was lost?
2. What claim could a reviewer challenge as newly invented?
3. What would a hurried reader misunderstand?
4. What action is still ambiguous?

Repair any answer that reveals a material problem.
