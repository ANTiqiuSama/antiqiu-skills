# Designing Studies

Turn a scoped research question into a useful prospective experiment, benchmark or simulation. Optimize for identifiable effects and informative comparisons, not methodological complexity. For analysis of already collected data, use the relevant analysis workflow.

Define the hypothesis, population/unit, quantity to estimate, relevant comparator, outcomes and observations that would refute the hypothesis. Explain those quantities in the problem's terms. If a missing choice materially changes the design, ask and continue independent work; otherwise state reasonable assumptions.

Choose controls, assignment and timing appropriate to the question. Address plausible confounding, leakage, selection and measurement bias. Justify sample or compute budget using precision, detectable effect and clustering where relevant; a small exploratory pilot can estimate uncertain assumptions.

For confirmation, pre-specify primary analysis, exclusions, missing-data handling and stopping rules. For exploration, record configurations, data use and all outcomes without requiring a full confirmatory protocol before trying the idea. Check practical feasibility and actual consent/privacy/review requirements when the study involves them.

For a design-only request, return a concise design in chat: hypothesis, inputs, comparisons, measurement, budget and stopping. When implementation or testing is requested, continue to a runnable experiment, simulation or evaluation with actual results within the authorized scope; a design document alone is not completion. Save only necessary configuration and reproducibility notes. Add power calculations or ethics detail when they affect validity or execution, and distinguish exploration from confirmation.
